The Beginning

Why I Chose Dentistry

Dentistry is often seen as a career focused mainly on fixing teeth and performing procedures. Through research and thirty-plus hours of job shadowing, I discovered it is far more complex — a profession that combines scientific knowledge, technical skill, communication, business management, and ethical decision-making every single day.

I became interested in dentistry because it sits at the intersection of healthcare, hands-on work, problem-solving, and human connection. I wanted to go beyond what patients experience in the chair and understand what the career truly demands from the people who practise it.

🔬

Healthcare & Science

Dentistry is deeply rooted in anatomy, biology, pathology, and pharmacology — requiring the scientific rigour of medicine.

🤝

Human Connection

Patients often arrive anxious or in pain. Managing their emotions and building trust is as important as any clinical skill.

⚙️

Hands-On Problem Solving

Every case is different. Dentists must think critically, adapt in real time, and make high-stakes decisions under pressure.

📊

Business & Leadership

Running a private clinic means managing staff, finances, billing, and operations — a side most patients never see.

The Pathway

Education & Training in Canada

Becoming a dentist is one of the most demanding academic journeys in the Canadian healthcare system — typically eight or more years of focused study before independent practice.

1

Undergraduate Degree

3–4 Years

Most students complete a bachelor's degree, often in a science-related field. Prerequisite courses typically include biology, chemistry, organic chemistry, and sometimes physics. Strong grades and extracurricular involvement are both essential.

Biology Chemistry Organic Chemistry Volunteer Experience
2

Dental Aptitude Test (DAT)

Before Application

Administered by the Canadian Dental Association, the DAT assesses scientific knowledge, perceptual ability, reading comprehension, and problem-solving skills. Performance on this test is a critical admissions factor, alongside interviews that assess communication and professionalism.

CDA-Administered Perceptual Ability Scientific Reasoning
3

Dental School — DDS or DMD

4 Years

Dental school combines classroom science with rigorous clinical training. Years one and two focus on anatomy, oral biology, pathology, and simulation labs. Years three and four shift to supervised clinical care — real patients, real procedures, real decisions.

Anatomy Oral Biology Pathology Clinical Practice CDAC Accredited
4

NDEB Certification & Provincial Licensing

After Graduation

Graduates must pass the National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB) process before practising. In British Columbia, licensing is overseen by the BC College of Oral Health Professionals. International graduates face additional bridging requirements to meet Canadian standards.

NDEB Exam BC College of Oral Health Professionals Provincial Licensing
5

Optional Specialization

2–6 Additional Years

General dentists may pursue specialization in orthodontics, oral surgery, periodontics, endodontics, and more. Specialists must pass the National Dental Specialty Examination administered by the Royal College of Dentists of Canada.

Orthodontics Oral Surgery Periodontics Endodontics
∞

Continuing Education

Throughout Career

Dentists must complete Continuing Education (CE) courses throughout their career in order to keep their licence. As new techniques and technology emerge, staying up to date is not a choice — it is a requirement built into the profession.

The Daily Rhythm

A Day in the Clinic

From the morning huddle to final charting, the day at a private dental clinic is highly structured and consistently unpredictable.

☀️
8:00 AM

Team Huddle

The dentists, hygienists, CDAs, and front desk meet to review the day's schedule, flag complex cases, and prepare for anxious patients.

📋
8:30 AM

Chart Review & Prep

The dentist reviews X-rays, treatment notes, and charts before the first patient arrives. CDAs prepare treatment rooms and sterilize instruments.

🦷
9:00 AM+

Patient Appointments

A mix of check-ups, fillings, crowns, extractions, orthodontics, and emergencies — the dentist moves fluidly between rooms, adjusting to each case in real time.

📝
Between Appointments

Charting & Admin

After each appointment, the dentist reviews their patients' notes. Free time goes to reviewing upcoming cases, ordering supplies, or handling business tasks.

🌙
End of Day

Reset & Prepare

Staff clean and reset treatment rooms, sterilize equipment, and review the next day's schedule. Planning ahead is what makes tomorrow run smoothly.

Observed Firsthand

Procedures & Technology

A selection of what I observed during my thirty-plus hours at Regehr Family Dental — each one revealing a layer of dentistry I hadn't anticipated.

🦷

Simple Extraction

Using an elevator instrument — a small metal tool that fits between tooth and gum to break the periodontal ligament — followed by forceps removal. The dentist explained each step throughout the procedure.

Routine Procedure
⚡

Surgical Extraction

When root shape made standard removal impossible, the tooth was sectioned before extraction. The more complex approach required additional instruments and clinical judgment made entirely in real time.

Complex Case
🔬

Dental Laser Filling

Instead of a drill, a dental laser was used to remove the initial decay — significantly reducing noise and vibration. Not yet universal due to equipment cost, but increasingly common for select restorations.

Emerging Technology
📱

Digital Crown Scan

The CDA completed a full intraoral digital scan before the dentist entered the room, allowing immediate review and treatment discussion with the patient.

Digital Workflow
📜

Informed Consent Process

Before procedures like extractions or implants, the CDA or the denitist walked the patient through a consent form — explaining risks, benefits, and alternatives — to ensure the patient had a clear understanding.

Patient Care
🤝

Anxiety Management

The dentist paused before each new step — "you will feel pressure, but not pain" — and deliberately slowed speech inside the treatment room even when running behind. Patient tension visibly reduced each time.

Communication Technique
Unexpected Discoveries

What Surprised Me Most

These were the moments that challenged my assumptions about what dentistry actually is.

🎭

It's a Performance Under Pressure

The dentist deliberately slowed down inside the treatment room even when the schedule was running behind — a conscious act of emotional management, not natural ease.

👥

Teamwork Drives Everything

The CDA had the scan done before the dentist walked in. The hygienist had identified the issues. The front desk had handled the forms. The dentist arrived to a prepared room, not a blank slate.

📊

Dentists Run Businesses

Payroll, supply orders, marketing, insurance reimbursements, overhead — dental school teaches procedures, not the business skills that determine whether a practice survives.

⚖️

Ethics Are Tested Daily

The pressure to upsell procedures exists in every competitive market. Recommending only what is necessary — and billing accurately — is an active ethical choice made repeatedly, every day.

The Hard Parts

Challenges in Private Practice

Dentistry is demanding on multiple fronts simultaneously. These are the pressures shaping the profession today.

01

Staffing Shortages & Burnout

Finding and retaining trained dental assistants, hygienists, and specialists is increasingly difficult. Staff shortages increase workload on remaining team members, accelerating burnout in a profession that is already emotionally demanding.

02

The Business Education Gap

Dental school graduates are clinically prepared but professionally underprepared. Payroll, financial management, HR, and marketing receive virtually no coverage — yet they determine whether a practice thrives or struggles.

03

Pressure to Perform Perfectly

Even a slightly off filling can cause noticeable bite problems. Dentists work in an area where precision is non-negotiable and outcomes are affected by factors entirely outside their control, such as patient genetics, habits, healing ability.

04

Financial Pressure & Student Debt

Opening a clinic requires significant capital, because equipment, rent, staff, and regulatory compliance are all costly. Many graduates carry large student loans, and income is often unstable in the early years of independent practice.

05

Corporatization of the Industry

Large dental corporations benefit from bulk supply purchasing and marketing scale. Independent clinics face unequal competitive conditions, particularly in markets where patient choice is broad and advertising is expensive.

06

The Canadian Dental Care Plan

The federal CDCP may extend access for uninsured Canadians with household incomes under $90,000 — potentially reaching nine million people — but reimbursement rates below private insurance levels concern clinics with high overhead.

Healthcare Context

The Dental Healthcare System in Canada

Unlike physician care, dental services in Canada operate almost entirely outside universal public coverage, creating a system where access depends heavily on income, insurance, and location.

🏛️

Mostly Private, Mostly Uninsured

Dental clinics operate as private businesses. There is no universal dental coverage equivalent to provincial health insurance for medical care. Most patients pay through employer benefit plans or out-of-pocket. In 2022, approximately one-third of Canadians had no dental insurance at all.

🍁

Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP)

Launched in late 2023, the CDCP targets uninsured Canadians with household incomes below $90,000. When fully implemented, it aims to extend coverage to those currently without access.

9M
Canadians expected to benefit when the CDCP is fully implemented
🌿

Indigenous Coverage (NIHB)

Indigenous Services Canada provides dental coverage for eligible First Nations and Inuit individuals through the Non-Insured Health Benefits program, covering preventive, restorative, and medically necessary orthodontic care.

💼

Private Insurance

Most insured Canadians receive coverage through employer benefit plans administered by large insurers — Sun Life, Manulife, Canada Life, and Desjardins — with significant variation in what each plan covers. Many exclude crowns, implants, and orthodontics.

📄

BC Fee Guide

The BC Dental Association publishes an annual fee guide listing suggested prices for every procedure. Insurance companies base reimbursements on this guide, though clinics may charge above or below the suggested rate depending on overhead and business model.

📍

Access by Location & Income

Rural communities often have fewer clinics and longer travel times, while urban areas have higher operating costs that are passed on to patients. Access remains deeply tied to location and financial circumstance.

Looking Back

Conclusion & Reflection

Dentistry is much more than simply fixing teeth. It is a challenging healthcare profession that requires scientific knowledge, technical skill, ethical responsibility, and genuine human connection.

Before beginning this project, I mostly viewed dentistry as a technical healthcare profession focused on procedures and oral health. Through observation and research, I discovered that dentistry also involves communication, leadership, business management, ethics, and emotional resilience.

The specific moments I observed during shadowing have stayed with me more than anything I read. Watching the dentist explain each step of an extraction to a visibly nervous patient — and seeing that patient relax as a result — made the importance of communication feel real in a way that no textbook fully could. Seeing the CDA prepare a room and walk a patient through consent forms before the dentist arrived showed me how much every team member matters, not just the dentist.

This project strengthened my interest in healthcare and helped me understand the skills I will need to develop if I choose to pursue dentistry or another health profession in the future. Communication, emotional control, time management, attention to detail, and professionalism are qualities I want to continue building regardless of the specific path I take.
